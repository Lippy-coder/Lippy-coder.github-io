## 1. Who is the developer, what is their background, and what have they said publicly about the method, data sources and alert logic?

### Takeaway
The app is built by Andy Stables, a part-time crofter, web developer and amateur aurora photographer in Glendale, Isle of Skye. He says he built his method empirically: he photographed substorms every night from Skye and matched the photos against satellite (L1 solar wind) and ground-magnetometer data. The method that results is a magnetometer-driven "substorm strength" in nT, which is a real-time "now" signal, with propagated L1 IMF/Bz used as the "coming" signal. He openly rejects Kp and the OVATION model.

It describes Andy Stables as a part-time crofter whose app "accurately predicts when the Northern Lights can be seen", with "over 1,000 users logging on each night". He and his wife had moved to Glendale full-time about 10 years earlier (so around 2007). He was not a childhood stargazer: "I got into star gazing by accident…", "I'd seen the auroras a few times…" — [Press and Journal](https://www.pressandjournal.co.uk/fp/news/1140162/skye-crofter-creates-viral-aurora-app/) (snippet only; the page could not be fetched).
- **[USER / news]** The same article reports that he spent about a year photographing substorms and correlating the photos with satellite and magnetometer data in order to predict aurora — [Press and Journal](https://www.pressandjournal.co.uk/fp/news/1140162/skye-crofter-creates-viral-aurora-app/) (snippet).
- **[DEV]** His Flickr profile (joined January 2014) describes him as a "crofter, web developer and amateur photographer living in Glendale on the Isle of Skye" and as admin of the Glendale Skye and Glendale Skye Auroras Facebook pages — [Flickr: About Andy Stables](https://www.flickr.com/people/glendaleskye/).

- **[DEV]** The magnetometer feeds credited are the Tromsø Geophysical Observatory, the Swedish Institute of Space Physics, the USGS, the Finnish Meteorological Institute, the British Geological Survey and Natural Resources Canada — [aurora-alerts.uk](https://aurora-alerts.uk/) (search summary).

- **[DEV]** Kp and K values are described as "totally unreliable". The stated reason is that the most widely used aggregator does not use the most recent estimated Kp (EPK) for the current 3-hour period and instead shows the final value from the last complete period. The developer says the app's "substorm strength" indicator "gives the most accurate approximation to visible aurora anywhere in the world" — [aurora-alerts.uk FAQs](https://aurora-alerts.uk/faqs.php) (FAQ content as surfaced by search; included because it explains the method).
- **[DEV]** The same FAQ says Norwegian magnetometers "provide the best correlation to visible auroras" compared with UK ones. It says UK magnetometers often "fail to detect activity or react a couple of hours after the light show started" — [aurora-alerts.uk FAQs](https://aurora-alerts.uk/faqs.php) (search summary).
- **[DEV]** On his phases photo-guide, the developer links visual aurora to magnetometer signatures. In the growth phase the diffuse aurora often coincides with a rise in magnetometer readings, and the expansion phase begins when the magnetometers "dip sharply". He gives an example in which he sent a text alert at 21:28 UT, the moment the magnetometers began to dip sharply — [glendaleskye.com aurora-phases](https://www.glendaleskye.com/aurora-phases.php).
- **[DEV]** Developer guidance as surfaced in search: when the Bz arriving at Earth "suddenly flips negative, a substorm begins to develop. Aurora doesn't happen right away" (he uses the analogy of a rechargeable torch charging). He also says to "ignore all the IMF/Bz predictions once a substorm is in its expansion phase", because substorm strength is then the thing to watch — search summary of [aurora-alerts.uk](https://aurora-alerts.uk/) / [Chasing Aurora Australis guide](https://chasingaurora.com.au/glendale-app-your-ultimate-guide/).
- **[DEV]** The FAQ claims the app "can accurately predict the time that activity will start 1 to 2 hours in advance" and alerts "with 100% accuracy when the aurora is about to start" — [aurora-alerts.uk FAQs](https://aurora-alerts.uk/faqs.php) (marketing claim, not independently verified).
- **[DEV]** The Iceland version was built with Caroline Weir of Aurora Iceland, who is based in Reykjavik and runs the Aurora Iceland Facebook group. It was "only made possible thanks to many hours of detailed observation and recording" by her, which mirrors his UK observation work. The app auto-switches mode when the user arrives in a new country — [aurora-alerts.uk user guide](https://aurora-alerts.uk/user-guide.php); [Aurora Iceland](https://www.auroraiceland.uk/aurora-borealis-iceland/fraserburgh-aurora-c-caroline-weir/).
- **[DEV]** The app is a web app (PWA) installed from the browser and is "not in any app store". It now covers the UK, Iceland, Scandinavia, Canada, the US, Australia and New Zealand — [aurora-alerts.uk](https://aurora-alerts.uk/); [Telegram channel description](https://t.me/s/GlendaleApp).

### Inferences
- **[INF]** The method appears to have two layers. (a) A *driver* layer: L1 IMF (DSCOVR/ACE via SWPC), propagated to Earth arrival times, which rates how good the IMF is and when negative-Bz intervals will arrive. (b) A *response* layer: a "substorm strength" index in nT built from high-latitude ground-magnetometer H-component drops. This looks AL/AE-like, probably taken from the most negative station in a relevant sector (Tromsø and other Norwegian stations for UK users), and phase detection uses its slope (rise = growth, sharp dip = expansion onset). Alerts seem to key mainly on layer (b).
- **[INF]** The thresholds were calibrated empirically: years of nightly photographs from Skye (and Weir's Icelandic logs) matched against magnetometer values. So the nT-to-visibility mapping is region-specific. This fits the auto-switching per country and the developer's preference for Norwegian over UK magnetometers for Scottish visibility.
- **[INF]** The "1–2 hours in advance" claim probably comes from the IMF layer: about 30–60 min L1-to-Earth propagation, plus the growth-phase loading time after a southward turning.

## 2. How do experienced chasers explain reading the Glendale screens (which values and thresholds matter, how Next/Now/Past are used, what % negative and theta tell you)?

### Takeaway
Third-party guides concentrate on the substorm-strength number (more negative = stronger aurora now) and the phase labels (growth / expansion / recovery). For the IMF chart, they tell you to look for Bz pushed down toward −Bt, below the shaded "ambient" band. Theta is the clock angle (best when near 180°, meaning due south). I found no third-party explanation of "percent negative" or the Next/Now/Past windows.

### Cited Findings
- **[USER]** The Chasing Aurora Australis guide "Glendale App – Your Ultimate Guide" is about 501 days old as of Oct 2026 (approx. mid-2025). It explains that the number at the top (e.g. "−209 nT") is the "substorm strength" and tells you "how strong the Aurora is in the sky right now". It describes the phases as: growth = sky "charging up like a battery", expansion = "charged-up energy is now being released as Aurora", recovery = "Aurora is fading". It says falling (more negative) is good and rising is bad — [Chasing Aurora Australis](https://chasingaurora.com.au/glendale-app-your-ultimate-guide/) (snippet).
- **[USER/DEV mixed]** On the 24-hour IMF chart, Bt is plotted in black and Bz in red. "You want the middle line (Bz) to be below the blue shaded (ambient) area and as close to the black line as possible." A 24-hour theta plot shows the clock angle (θ) "which allows you to see the periods when the IMF was best aligned" — search summary of [Chasing Aurora Australis](https://chasingaurora.com.au/glendale-app-your-ultimate-guide/) and [aurora-alerts.uk](https://aurora-alerts.uk/) (the snippet did not make clear which site each sentence came from).
- **[Reference, not Glendale]** Standard clock-angle convention: 0° = IMF Bz north, 180° = Bz south — [Australian Bureau of Meteorology SWS](https://www.sws.bom.gov.au/Category/Solar/Solar%20Conditions/Solar%20Wind%20Clock%20Angle/Solar%20Wind%20Clock%20Angle.php). It is unconfirmed that Glendale uses the same scale.
- **[USER]** Location permission matters: "if you don't allow access to your location, the forecasts will be wrong" — search summary of the [Chasing Aurora Australis guide](https://chasingaurora.com.au/glendale-app-your-ultimate-guide/).
- **[USER]** A US chaser, Melissa F. Kaelin (Beyond the Aurora Substack, about early 2025), recommends watching Telegram for "ORANGE ALERTS or stronger" to "track powerful substorms". She lists this next to NOAA SWPC, ACE real-time solar wind and the Kyoto real-time AE index — [Beyond the Aurora: Chasing Savvy favourite tools](https://beyondtheaurora.substack.com/p/chasing-savvy-favorite-aurora-chasing). She also wrote "Understanding the Trending Set of Aurora Alerts" (2025-01-26) — [kaelinart.com](https://kaelinart.com/2025/01/26/understanding-the-trending-set-of-aurora-alerts/) (could not be fetched; content unknown).
- **[USER]** A 2019-era photography guide says Stables "understands the graphs and charts better than we ever will, he goes out every night with his camera for a look" — [bulloch.photography](https://bulloch.photography/aurora-hunting) (snippet).
- **[USER]** An auroraphotographysettings.com blog post and a paid "Aurora – Educational Series – The Glendale App" product exist. The search summary describes them as promotional ("real-time data, personalized alerts"), with no IMF-screen detail surfaced — [blog](https://www.auroraphotographysettings.com/post/unleash-the-power-of-the-glendale-aurora-app-your-ultimate-aurora-chase-companion); [product](https://www.auroraphotographysettings.com/product-page/aurora-educational-series-the-glendale-app).

### Inferences
- **[INF]** "Percent negative" is most likely the share of minutes in a window (Next / Now / Past) during which propagated Bz < 0. Theta for each window is likely the mean or median clock angle. "Next" would be the L1 data not yet arrived at Earth (propagated arrival within about 30–60 min), "Now" the interval currently arriving, and "Past" the interval that has already arrived. Read together, they tell you whether the magnetosphere is loading (Past/Now) and whether loading will continue (Next). This is consistent with the developer's statement about "negative Bz periods… what times they will be hitting Earth and how strong". The exact definitions are unverified.
- **[INF]** The "blue shaded ambient area" suggests Bz is judged against a background band (perhaps ±some fraction of Bt, or the recent typical range) rather than just against zero.

## 3. What do users report about when alerts fire, lead time, false alarms, and how Glendale compares with other apps?

### Takeaway
Alerts are colour-coded tiers (yellow → orange → red → purple/major/severe/extreme), sent via Telegram, X and web push. They are tied to the substorm tracker detecting a substorm moving into the expansion phase. Users mostly praise them, but the evidence is anecdotal. I found no systematic false-alarm or lead-time data. The developer's own example shows an alert sent at the moment the magnetometers dip sharply, so alerts are near-real-time "it's happening now/imminent" signals rather than hours-ahead forecasts.

### Cited Findings
- **[DEV]** Alerts are colour-coded from yellow (minor) through orange and red to major, severe and extreme. Levels apply by region: northern Scotland reaches lower levels first, while RED applies to the whole UK and Ireland — [aurora-alerts.uk user guide](https://aurora-alerts.uk/user-guide.php) (search summary).
- **[DEV]** Example Telegram alert wording: "ORANGE ALERT: Moderate geomagnetic substorm in progress". Posts contain the alert level, a link and a UTC timestamp — [Telegram: Aurora Alerts UK (Glendale App)](https://t.me/s/GlendaleApp) (snippet). The same alerts are mirrored on X as [@SkyeAuroras](https://x.com/SkyeAuroras).
- **[DEV]** "Activity Alerts" fire much more often and cover threshold crossings such as "Major activity level reached" plus trusted-user reports and photo uploads. In desktop "alarm mode" the app "will start bleeping as soon as it detects a substorm heading into expansion phase". iPhone users do not get push alerts and must use Telegram — [aurora-alerts.uk user guide](https://aurora-alerts.uk/user-guide.php) (search summary).
- **[DEV]** Text alert example timed to the sharp magnetometer dip at 21:28 UT — [glendaleskye.com aurora-phases](https://www.glendaleskye.com/aurora-phases.php). A separate Glendale write-up mentions a warning posted on the Glendale Skye Auroras Facebook page at 20:00 GMT (search summary, page not identified precisely; see [Glendale Skye Auroras Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/GlendaleSkyeAuroras/)).
- **[USER]** Astro Bob (Bob King) wrote "What's the best aurora app?" in the Duluth News Tribune, about early 2026 by snippet age. He calls Glendale "a lot of fun because it's so interactive". It pulls in live reports and photos from observers in several countries, users tap to report cloudy / clear no aurora / aurora visible, it has zero ads, and it has "some 210,000 users at present". He compares it with SpaceWeatherLive ("probably the most comprehensive") and My Aurora Forecast & Alerts ("one of the easiest to use") — [Duluth News Tribune](https://www.duluthnewstribune.com/lifestyle/astro-bob/astro-bob-whats-the-best-aurora-app); [mirror at West Central Tribune](https://www.wctrib.com/lifestyle/astro-bob/astro-bob-whats-the-best-aurora-app) (snippets).
- **[USER]** Users on Stargazers Lounge reported "some good success with the Glendale Web app" and noted it is "installable onto your phone so it works like a normal app" — [Stargazers Lounge thread 417618](https://stargazerslounge.com/topic/417618-best-websiteandroid-app-for-aurora-notifications/); see also [thread 414009](https://stargazerslounge.com/topic/414009-aurora-prediction-apps/) (snippets).
- **[USER]** A blogger reported getting notifications from Glendale and AuroraWatch UK "all within minutes of each other" during one event — search summary, source page not identified (possibly [thenoblegasbag](https://thenoblegasbag.wordpress.com/)). Low reliability.
- **[USER]** In 2023 comments on Shutter Muse, "I've always used the Glendale App", and a cruise passenger recommended it — [Shutter Muse](https://shuttermuse.com/best-aurora-forecasting-apps-for-northern-lights-photography/). A Rick Steves forum post calls it run by "a well known aurora expert" — [Rick Steves forum](https://community.ricksteves.com/travel-forum/scotland/northern-lights-viewing-opportunities-in-october). Another source says it has "almost obtained cult status within the Aurora photography community" (search summary).
- **[Comparison, other app's own docs]** AuroraWatch UK uses real-time UK magnetometer data processed about every 3 minutes, with "a necessary delay before alerts are sent since the data needs to 'settle down'" — [AuroraWatch UK App Store](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/aurorawatch-uk-aurora-alerts/id946141347); [Aurora Watch (UK) Play Store](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.smallbouldering.aurorawatchuk&hl=en_GB). Hello Aurora offers substorm notifications with a user-set minimum intensity (search summary of [Hello Aurora](https://hello-aurora.com/)).
- **[USER/press]** Sky at Night Magazine mentions that Glendale "claims to be the most accurate aurora forecasting and alerting app in the world" and frames this as the app's own claim — [Sky at Night](https://www.skyatnightmagazine.com/advice/how-predict-aurora) (snippet).

### Inferences
- **[INF]** Alert firing logic most likely works like this: substorm-strength (nT) crosses tier thresholds AND/OR the phase detector flags an expansion onset (sharp negative slope). Thresholds are scaled by the user's region/latitude, since the same disturbance gives yellow for northern Scotland but needs red for the whole UK. This would make alerts largely free of Kp-style false alarms (they react to measured ground disturbance) but give short lead time, minutes rather than hours.
- **[INF]** Glendale probably beats AuroraWatch UK on timing because it uses higher-latitude Norwegian stations (which respond earlier and more strongly to the expanding auroral electrojet) and avoids a "settle" delay.

### Gaps
- No quantitative false-alarm rate or lead-time statistics from any source.
- No Reddit or Facebook-group threads comparing alert timing were retrievable.
- Nothing was found on whether IMF conditions alone (e.g. Bz south for X minutes) trigger an alert. The evidence points to magnetometer-triggered alerts plus user-report "activity alerts".

## 4. Any mention of ground magnetometers, local/regional alerts, location settings, or a combined "go/no-go" score?

### Takeaway
Ground magnetometers are central (six national networks are credited, and Norwegian stations are preferred for UK visibility). Alerts are regional, and the app uses device location (auto-switching country mode). It has a crowd-sourced report layer. I found no evidence of a single combined go/no-go score; colour-coded IMF quality plus tiered substorm alerts play that role.

### Cited Findings
- **[DEV]** Magnetometer sources: Tromsø, IRF Sweden, USGS, FMI, BGS and NRCan — [aurora-alerts.uk](https://aurora-alerts.uk/).
- **[DEV]** Alert tiers are region-dependent (northern Scotland first; RED = all UK and Ireland) — [aurora-alerts.uk user guide](https://aurora-alerts.uk/user-guide.php).
- **[DEV/USER]** The app auto-switches country mode on arrival, and location access is needed or "forecasts will be wrong" — [aurora-alerts.uk user guide](https://aurora-alerts.uk/user-guide.php); [Chasing Aurora Australis](https://chasingaurora.com.au/glendale-app-your-ultimate-guide/).
- **[DEV]** "Simple, colour-coded indications of how good the Interplanetary Magnetic Field is looking" — [glendaleskye.com](https://www.glendaleskye.com/aurora-alerts-app.php).
- **[USER]** Crowd reports (cloudy / clear-no-aurora / aurora visible) and photos from trusted users are part of the app — [Astro Bob, Duluth News Tribune](https://www.duluthnewstribune.com/lifestyle/astro-bob/astro-bob-whats-the-best-aurora-app).

### Inferences
- **[INF]** A replication would need: per-region station selection, a per-region nT-to-visibility calibration, location-based region assignment, and a separate IMF "quality" colour (from % negative, Bz magnitude relative to Bt, and theta). There would be no single blended score.

### Gaps
- Station-to-region mapping and the exact per-region thresholds are not public in any source found.

## 5. Any reported changes in method over time?

### Takeaway
The evidence shows growth in coverage and delivery rather than documented changes to the core method. It went from a Skye-only tool (about 2012–2017, about 1,000 nightly users) to a multi-country PWA (Iceland via Caroline Weir, then Scandinavia, North America, Australia/NZ) with Telegram/X alerts, and about 210,000 users by about 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **[DEV]** Daily research on Skye since September 2012 — [aurora-alerts.uk](https://aurora-alerts.uk/).
- **[USER/news]** About Jan 2017: over 1,000 users nightly — [Press and Journal](https://www.pressandjournal.co.uk/fp/news/1140162/skye-crofter-creates-viral-aurora-app/).
- **[DEV]** Iceland version added with Caroline Weir's observation records — [aurora-alerts.uk user guide](https://aurora-alerts.uk/user-guide.php).
- **[DEV]** Coverage now spans the UK, Iceland, Scandinavia, Canada, the US, Australia and NZ — [Telegram channel](https://t.me/s/GlendaleApp).
- **[USER]** About 2026: "some 210,000 users" — [Astro Bob](https://www.duluthnewstribune.com/lifestyle/astro-bob/astro-bob-whats-the-best-aurora-app).
- **[DEV]** The satellite list includes both DSCOVR and ACE — [glendaleskye.com](https://www.glendaleskye.com/aurora-alerts-app.php). [Reference] DSCOVR is now the primary real-time L1 source and ACE is the backup — search summary of third-party explainers (e.g. [Sky & Telescope](https://skyandtelescope.org/stargazing-and-observing/keep-track-of-the-aurora/)).

### Inferences
- **[INF]** The original 2012–2017 calibration was a single-site empirical calibration (Skye photos vs magnetometers). Expansion to other countries probably required new local calibrations, as was done for Iceland with Weir's logs. Any change in L1 feeds (e.g. a SWPC switch from DSCOVR to newer L1 assets in 2025–26) would affect the IMF screen but not the magnetometer-based alerts.
